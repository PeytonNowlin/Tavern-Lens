Checked October 2, 2026. Focus: additional expert/community composition and leveling knowledge outside the Firestone, HSReplay, card-metadata and simulator sources investigated separately. Research only; no application changes, provider outreach, account access or guide mirror.

## Finding

This bounded search did **not establish a fresh, independently maintained, licensed structured expert feed**. It did establish a real public curvesheet with actionable rows, an independently authored guide with commitment sections, and that Jeef's former independent site now leads to the HSReplay source already under investigation. This is useful evidence about where to seek reviewed annotations, not another current production feed.

Policy 10 already selects strategic directions. The missing contribution from these sources would be conditional guidance inside a direction: the minimum working engine, acceptable alternatives, the resource prerequisites for a payoff, and when to delay or abandon the route. More final-board card membership would duplicate existing information.

## What the curvesheet actually adds

The rendered sheet supplies an action schedule rather than composition membership. Its notes encode economy/hero-power requirements, fast and slow alternatives, and fallback behavior. The companion [Basic Curve page](https://www.bgcurvesheet.com/curves/main-curves/basic-curve) has separate turn instructions and a branch to another curve when the offered shop is poor. Those are useful shapes for contextual leveling advice. The inspected page has historical card examples and no verifiable patch-review timestamp; its current availability is not evidence that its recommendations remain valid.

No complete hero-to-curve mapping, current trinket/quest modifiers, survival-conditioned curve dataset, outcome sample counts or machine-readable API was established. A curvesheet must not become an unconditional “level on this turn” rule.

## Proposed independently authored annotation layer

Use current game mechanics and reviewed decision examples to write our own small, versioned rule set. Keep source references for comparison; do not import entire guide prose or equate an accessible page with permission to redistribute it. If exact provider content is needed in the app, establish its permitted use separately.

Each reviewed row should contain:

- **Provenance:** author/reviewer, source reference where relevant, game build/patch, review date, supported mode/mechanic, and an explicit content-use status.
- **Engine:** enabling-card alternatives, resource production, multiplier/payoff roles, minimum working combination, board/hand space needed, and cards that provide temporary tempo.
- **Decision conditions:** what makes the line viable now; gold, health, tier and timing constraints; what to acquire next; when a payoff is premature; and the condition that changes or ends the plan.
- **Leveling branches:** feasible action sequence, required economy/hero power, a strong-shop alternative, and a stabilization alternative. Validate legality and current upgrade costs against the local engine.
- **Evaluation cases:** complete decision snapshots with an approved plan, acceptable alternatives, and a reviewed reason. Include failed transitions and weak boards, not just successful end states.

Start with the actual recurring gaps in recent local requests and two or three affected directions, rather than annotating every possible build. Review the rules against whole-game held-out decisions before increasing their score weight. A patch should trigger revalidation of affected rules; keeping a card in the pool does not establish that its role or commitment timing is unchanged.

The practical opportunity from this lane is a better structured review process for strategic knowledge. The checked sources do not supply measured current decision values, a ready-to-use action policy, or verified permission for a guide mirror.
